Practice Areas

Estate planning, made plain.

Most families come to me to put their affairs in good order — or to settle an estate after a loss. I keep the process clear, unhurried, and free of jargon.

Wills & Trusts

Simple and complex wills, revocable living trusts, and joint wills — drafted to say exactly what you mean, so your wishes are honored.

Probate

Guiding families and personal representatives through Oregon probate after a death — patiently, and at a fair fee, when you have enough to carry already.

Powers of Attorney

Durable powers of attorney and advance directives so a trusted person can act for you — should illness or age ever make that necessary.

Guardianship & Conservatorship

Standing up protections for a minor or an adult who can no longer manage their own affairs, with care for the whole family involved.

Real Estate & Deeds

Purchases and sales, promissory notes, and trust deeds — often the companion piece to a sound estate plan.

Business & Beyond

Forming an LLC or corporation, business sales, and general counsel — plus family law and personal injury matters when they arise.

About Wendell

An old-country lawyer, by design.

Wendell L. Belknap has practiced law in Oregon City since being admitted to the bar in 1985, after graduating cum laude from Lewis & Clark Law School. A sixth-generation Oregonian, he built his practice to feel less like a firm and more like the office of an old country lawyer — the kind who knows your name and answers the phone himself.

That means no gatekeepers and no meter running before you've even said hello. When you call, you reach Wendell. He'll listen, tell you plainly what your options are, and give you an honest read on whether hiring an attorney is worth the cost in your situation.

For estate planning and probate especially — matters that arrive at tender moments — that candor and steadiness are the whole point.

Why plan now

A good plan is a gift to the people you love.

1

Spare your family the hard questions

A clear will and durable power of attorney answer the questions your family would otherwise face at the worst possible time.

2

Keep it out of a courtroom where you can

The right plan can simplify — or avoid — probate, keeping decisions in the family's hands rather than the court's.

3

Work with one attorney, start to finish

No handoffs to associates. The lawyer you meet is the lawyer who drafts your documents and picks up when you call.
